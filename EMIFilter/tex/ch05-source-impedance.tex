\chapter{Source Impedance}
\label{ch:source}

\begin{idea}
	The source is part of the filter. The same filter has a different $\Zo$ and
	$H$ on every source it sees, so the stability check has to be repeated for
	each one: the bus in service, and every test setup the device will be
	connected to.
\end{idea}

\section{Source models}

Three sources cover most cases. All models are for the DM loop.
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source-models}
	Z_{bus} = R_s + sL_s, \qquad
	Z_{ft} = R + sL_{lead} + \frac{1}{s\,(C_{ft}/2)}, \qquad
	Z_{x} = j\omega L_x + \frac{\omega L_x}{Q_x} + Z_{bus}.
\end{equation}

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Stiff bus.} A small resistance and the wiring inductance.
	\item \textbf{Feedthrough capacitors.} Conducted emissions setups often put
	      a large feedthrough capacitor from each line to the ground plane. The two
	      sit in series around the DM loop, so the filter sees $C_{ft}/2$. The
	      model holds above the frequency where the capacitors swamp the bench
	      supply behind them, around \qty{10}{\kHz} for \qty{10}{\micro\farad} parts.
	\item \textbf{Series injection transformer.} Conducted susceptibility tests
	      often inject audio-frequency ripple through a transformer whose secondary
	      is in series with the power line. With its primary unterminated, the
	      secondary looks like a large inductor with modest $Q$, modeled here at
	      constant $Q$.
\end{itemize}

\section{New resonances from the source}

\subsection{A capacitive source}

The source capacitance resonates with the filter's input inductance. The true
frequency sits between two bounding estimates:
\[
	f \approx \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{L_{in}\,C_{src}}}
	\ \text{to}\
	\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{L_{in}\,(C_{src} \text{ in series with } C_1 + C_2)}},
	\qquad L_{in} = L_{lk} + L_1 \parallel L_d + L_{lead}.
\]

\subsection{A large series inductance}

When the external inductance dominates, it forms a new low mode with all the
filter capacitance:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:series-l-mode}
	f_x = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{L_{tot}(C_1 + C_2)}}, \qquad
	R_0 = \sqrt{\frac{L_{tot}}{C_1 + C_2}}, \qquad
	\abs{\Zo}_{peak} \approx Q_x R_0,
\end{equation}
with $L_{tot} = L_x + L_{lk} + L_1 + L_2$. Because $L_x$ is large, $R_0$ is
large, and the peak can easily exceed $\abs{\Zin}$. This is a nasty failure,
because it needs no test signal: the filter, transformer and converter can
oscillate as soon as the device powers up.

\begin{idea}
	A damping leg across an inductor damps only resonances that inductor takes
	part in. A leg across a shunt capacitor damps any upstream inductance
	resonating with that capacitor, \emph{including inductance you do not own}.
\end{idea}

To size an $R_d$--$C_d$ leg across $C_2$ for the series-inductance mode, use
\zcref{eq:rd-cd-opt} with $L = L_{tot}$ and $C = C_1 + C_2$. The other fix is
at the source: a few ohms across the transformer primary damps it directly.

\begin{examplebox}[label=ex:sources]{the candidate on three sources}
	The damped candidate from \zcref{ex:candidate-damped} ($n = 1$,
	$R_d = \qty{3.4}{\ohm}$) on:
	\begin{itemize}
		\item a stiff bus, $\qty{0.02}{\ohm} + s\cdot\qty{1}{\micro\henry}$;
		\item a feedthrough setup, two \qty{10}{\micro\farad} capacitors
		      (\qty{5}{\micro\farad} DM) with \qty{1.5}{\micro\henry} of lead
		      and \qty{0.01}{\ohm};
		\item the stiff bus plus a \qty{0.8}{\milli\henry}, $Q = 10$ transformer
		      secondary in series.
	\end{itemize}

	\begin{center}
		\small
		\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
			\toprule
			Source              & $\Zo$ peak                                    & Margin   & $\abs{H}$ at \qty{400}{\kHz} \\
			\midrule
			Ideal               & \qty{6.63}{\ohm} at \qty{10.9}{\kHz}          & 22.2~dB  & $-92.2$~dB                   \\
			Stiff bus           & \qty{6.75}{\ohm} at \qty{10.7}{\kHz}          & 22.0~dB  & $-92.5$~dB                   \\
			Feedthrough setup   & \qty{4.89}{\ohm} at \qty{63}{\kHz} (above \qty{10}{\kHz}) & 24.8~dB & $-92.7$~dB    \\
			Series transformer  & \qty{96.0}{\ohm} at \qty{2.08}{\kHz}          & $-1.1$~dB  & $-123.9$~dB                  \\
			\bottomrule
		\end{tabular}
	\end{center}

	\textbf{Feedthrough setup.} The \qty{5}{\micro\farad} source against
	$L_{in} = 2 + 19.5 + 1.5 = \qty{23}{\micro\henry}$ gives \qty{14.8}{\kHz}.
	With $C_1 + C_2$ in series with it, \qty{19.5}{\kHz}. The exact $H$ peak,
	$+4.6$~dB at \qty{17.3}{\kHz}, falls between the two. Stability is fine, but
	noise near \qty{17}{\kHz} is amplified rather than attenuated in this setup.

	\textbf{Series transformer.} From \zcref{eq:series-l-mode},
	$L_{tot} = \qty{0.846}{\milli\henry}$ against \qty{6.9}{\micro\farad}:
	\[
		f_x = \qty{2.08}{\kHz}, \qquad R_0 = \qty{11.1}{\ohm}, \qquad
		\abs{\Zo}_{peak} \approx 10 \times 11.1 = \qty{111}{\ohm}.
	\]
	The exact value is \qty{96.0}{\ohm}. That exceeds $\abs{\Zin} = \qty{85.0}{\ohm}$,
	so the source, filter and converter can oscillate.
	The damping leg cannot help, because 95\% of this mode's inductance is $L_x$
	and the leg shunts only $L_1$.
\end{examplebox}

\begin{examplebox}[label=ex:rd-cd]{an $R_d$--$C_d$ leg for the transformer}
	Size a leg across $C_2$ with \zcref{eq:rd-cd-opt}, using
	$R_0 = \qty{11.07}{\ohm}$ and the \qty{26.9}{\ohm} target:
	\[
		k = \left(\frac{26.9}{11.07}\right)^2 = 5.90, \qquad
		n_{min} = \frac{1 + \sqrt{1 + 4(5.90)}}{5.90} = 1.01, \qquad
		C_d = 1.01 \times \qty{6.9}{\micro\farad} = \qty{6.97}{\micro\farad}.
	\]
	Rounding down to a standard \qty{6.8}{\micro\farad} ($n = 0.99$) gives
	$R_{d,opt} = \qty{16.2}{\ohm}$. The sweep then shows \qty{20.4}{\ohm} at
	\qty{1.71}{\kHz} on the transformer, a 12.4~dB margin.

	On the stiff bus the low-mode peak drops to \qty{4.78}{\ohm} (25.0~dB),
	and $\abs{H}$ at \qty{400}{\kHz} is unchanged at $-92.5$~dB. A shunt leg costs
	no high-frequency attenuation, only board space for $C_d$.
\end{examplebox}
